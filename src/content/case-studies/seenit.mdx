<Section title="Background: Screenshots as a new language">

Gen Z takes 20–25 screenshots a day. They're how people save, think, shop, track, flirt, remember, and plan, it's their most honest digital behaviour and identity graph, yet they just sit hidden in camera albums.

Seenit started from a simple question: Can LLMs make sense of screenshots and turn them into a new form of self-expression?

</Section>

<Section title="Problem: People don't actually know what their friends are doing anymore">

Right now, friendship online is either:

1. a broadcast (too much)

2. a DM (too effortful)

Everything in between has disappeared. The small, in-between moments — the "this is what my day actually looked like" layer — are gone. Screenshots are the last unpolished signal we have. A massive, high-signal behaviour happening everywhere, with no way to share it.

</Section>

<Section title="Opportunity: A massive behaviour with zero product surface">

1. Screenshots have hit cultural peak: every Gen Z phone is already producing a feed. no one is harnessing it.

2. A way to stay close to friends with zero effort, no posting, and no self-presentation work. It restores the background texture friendships used to have.

3. AI is finally good enough to interpret messy inputs

4. A new social layer built on taste and intent: screenshots form a natural discovery layer that can power identity, recommendations, shopping, and cultural sense-making.

I saw an opportunity to build a consumer social layer built on a behaviour that already exists

</Section>

<Section title="Defining the bet: Can screenshots power response-driven sharing?">

To move from idea to product, I broke the problem into three behavioural pillars, signals that would tell us if screenshot-as-status could sustain a real loop:

1. **Expression:** can AI turn mundane screenshots into something share-worthy?
2. **Reaction:** do they spark replies or stories in existing channels?
3. **Usage:** can it become a sustained behaviour, not just a novelty?

</Section>

<Section title="First bet: Weekly summaries">

<CSYouTube 
  url="https://youtube.com/shorts/2FJmm4j1k6I"
  title="Weekly summaries experiment"
  caption="Testflight build"
/>

I partnered with an engineer to ship the v1 experiment: a sunday card generated from your screenshots.  In six weeks we went from a hypothesis to a live Testflight build. What I learned:

1. **Tone testing gave us intuition:** Each week we shifted the LLM's output (dry, playful, reflective) and gave users three cards to flick between. Meme-like captions consistently got the strongest response.
2. **Engagement came from comparison:** Shares happened in WhatsApp and group chats, not IG. It was for friends, not an audience.
3. **Summaries closed loops:** The best cards captured the the small, throwaway moments, the things people wouldn't usually share, but felt human and created reactions.

</Section>

<Section title="Pivot: Presence over summaries">

After three weeks, usage dropped. users didn't want a scrapbook; they wanted a sticker. When the original engineer stepped away, I led the pivot, defining a new product direction, rebuilding the product myself, and redesigning the experience around real-time presence.

The new version introduced lightweight, live-feeling status cards that updated as users moved through their day. The goal: make sharing feel as frictionless and honest as screenshotting itself. 

Seenit became a six-person friendship app, with each status interpreted from recent screenshots and shared as short POV updates. The content in the video above is generated from an LLM pipeline: statues, reactions, POVs. 

</Section>

<Section title="Adding a status">

Statuses were triggered without opening the app. When users get a push reminder, either because they haven't shared in a while, or a friend has, a back tap triggers a lightweight status, AI-generated from recent screenshots and shared to their squad. 

There's no post to craft, no caption to write, no feed to perform into. It's an interface that's passive in input, invisible in action, intelligent in output.

</Section>

<Section title="Extending presence through avatars">

To add identity beyond drops, I built an avatar system using a diffusion model. A user's IG photo generated a playful, personality-driven character used across profiles, widgets, invites and the feed.
