<Section>

Seenit turns phone screenshots into short, AI-generated status updates for close friends. V1 was a weekly Sunday card summarising your screenshots. But 70% of users had already moved on from the moments it described. I redesigned the system to reach users in the moment, not after the week had passed.

</Section>

<Section title="Lowering the cost of sharing">

Screenshotting is already instinctive; you do it for yourself, not for others. The product needed to preserve that quality while reminding users they had something worth sharing. I designed a push notification and live activity flow so users could share without opening the app or composing anything. The live activity means users aren't stranded on a loading screen while they wait.

</Section>

<Section title="Quality gate">

V1 hit a 20–30% share rate, the cards that got shared were specific, present-tense, and short enough to forward in a group chat. I used those as a proxy for whether a user would put their name to it, and built a scoring rubric to make that repeatable. Sensitive content filtered through on-device OCR before interpretation, anything below 24 points was auto-rejected and retried.

</Section>

<ScoringRubric />

<Section title="Regeneration">

I replaced traditional editing with a regenerate control. One tap asks the model for a different interpretation: a silent escape hatch for hallucinations. Users aren't locked into what the system initially inferred, so sharing requires no second-guessing.

</Section>

<Section title="Outcome and learnings">

1. The beta achieved a 20–30% share rate in trusted WhatsApp groups
1. The model's second attempt consistently outperformed the first: validating auto-reject and retry as the right architecture for the scoring rubric
1. Presence > identity. Friends care more about what you're in the middle of than how you present yourself

</Section>
