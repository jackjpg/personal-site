<Section title="Spotting the blind spot">

The final screen in the seller journey received 4× more page views than the homepage. Sellers kept returning, expecting updates that never came, they were anxious about a major asset they'd just sold. I asked myself: how does the lack of post-sale visibility impact user trust and satisfaction? To find out, I partnered with customer support and data teams to audit tickets and operational workflows: 

1. 100% of sales relied on whatsapp and phone calls, causing delays and cancellations

2. 56% of enquiries stemmed from sellers unsure of next steps

3. 25% cancellation rate and climbing

4. £3.5M+ annual revenue: At risk from poor experience

Sellers didn't know what to do, who they were waiting for, or whether the process was on track.



To expose why, I ran a cross-functional workshop mapping the entire post-sale flow. We mapped the current post-sale experience, annotated it with real seller pain points, and highlighted where ownership was fragmented or missing.

<Section title="Reframing the challenge">

How might we improve Motorway's post-sale experience from a 'black box' into a transparent, self-serve journey that reduces operational friction. This led to 3 product goals:

1. Clarity: Every user should know what they need to do, who they are waiting for, and why.

2. Visibility: Everything happening behind the scenes should be surfaced clearly.

3. Operational deflection: Reduce CS by turning common questions into product surfaces

</Section>

 

<Section title="Design vision and planning">

I created a vision storyboard to show what a transparent post-sale experience could feel like. It focused on all user types in the post-sale experience, showed updates appearing in real time, payments confirmed automatically, and communication moving in-app rather than over Whatsapp.

I presented the storyboard to the executive team, framing it as both a trust strategy and a growth opportunity. The head of product championed it, and together we aligned product leads around the vision, prioritising opportunity areas across squads, balancing short-term impact with scalable bets.



This alignment led to the creation of a dedicated post-sale pillar, where I partnered with a PM and six engineers to ship the MVP.

<Section title="Setting expectations">

To address the insights from UXR, our first launch was to start building trust during identity verification, as well as to prepare them for what's coming up. I made 2 improvements to build user confidence:

1. Redesigned the verification screen with clear timeframes, ownership, and next steps. 

2. Built on the signposting by adding an entry point that replaced a confusing dead-end with a clear explanation of what happens next.

This small piece of UX became a surprisingly important moment in the journey, closing a gap that previously left users uncertain and unsupported.

<Section title="The unified post-sale hub">

We replaced a fragmented, WhatsApp-driven experience with a single hub that became the source of truth once a car was sold. To mirror the seller's mental model, the page is designed around two anchors: whats happening now, what did I agree to.

<Section title="Making the journey visible">

The process after a sale was largely invisible. We delivered a milestone tracker that reflected the real operational flow:

- Linear progression tied to real backend events

- Time expectations to reduce uncertainty make the experience feel controlled 

- Progressive reveal of details, like transport partner, driver name, and collection window

<CSImage 
  src="/Project_post-sale/mw_post-sale_milestones.jpg"

Because most sellers had never sold through the Motorway before, we added short visual explainers for unfamiliar steps (Collection, inspection, payment release). These helped users build a clear mental model of the journey without digging through help articles, and each explainer aligned directly to a milestone in the timeline
